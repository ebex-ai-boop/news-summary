World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026.

Russia and Ukraine ended two days of US-mediated peace talks in Abu Dhabi agreeing a large prisoner swap but no ceasefire or territorial deal, Reuters reported; US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff head to Moscow on Thursday to see Vladimir Putin.

World leaders opened the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, where UN chief Antonio Guterres warned the slow response had put the world "on a path to destruction"; Turkey wants electricity to reach 35% of global energy by 2035, and the United States stayed away for a second year, Reuters reported.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said in Paris that economic pressure on Iran was working so well there was "really no need for major combat operations any longer", the New York Times reported, as Tehran awaited a US answer to its seven conditions and vowed to keep the Strait of Hormuz closed until then; three oil tankers caught fire on Tuesday after striking naval mines.

The US Justice Department charged three men it called Iranian-backed operatives over nearly 20 plots in the US and Europe, including a September plot at RAF Fairford in England; Treasury chief Scott Bessent moved to a military base after Iranian death threats, the New York Times reported. British police arrested a US Army officer with a NATO unit on suspicion of spying for Russia.

October US consumer prices rose 0.4% and stocks fell — the Dow 0.3%, the S&P 500 0.4% and the Nasdaq 0.7% — as Fed governor Christopher Waller floated a half-point December rate rise, lifting the 10-year Treasury yield above 5%.

The US House voted 211-204 to shelve an attempt to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, effectively killing it; President Donald Trump marked Veterans Day at Arlington National Cemetery.

The Democratic Republic of Congo confirmed 11,042 Ebola cases and at least 5,387 deaths, government data showed, making the Bundibugyo outbreak the second largest and second deadliest on record.
